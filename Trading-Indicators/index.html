Spot CVD by Exchange
Cumulative aggressive flow across Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, OKX, and Bybit — rolling 30-day history
Spot CVD (Cumulative Volume Delta) tracks net aggressive flow over time: buyer-initiated volume minus seller-initiated volume. Each line represents one exchange (Binance, Coinbase, Kraken, OKX, Bybit) and the dashed pink line is the aggregate across all five. Rising CVD suggests sustained market buy pressure; falling CVD suggests sustained sell pressure. When one exchange diverges from others (e.g. Coinbase CVD rising while Binance falls), it can signal regional or institutional flow differences. Traders watch for divergence vs price: price flat/down while aggregate CVD rises hints at absorption; price up with weakening CVD signals fragility.
Orderbook Balance Over Time
Are buyers or sellers stacking more passive liquidity? Green = buyers dominate, red = sellers dominate.
How to read this chart: The green shaded area shows periods where buy-side (bid) liquidity exceeds sell-side (ask) liquidity within 5% of the current price. The red shaded area shows the opposite. The white line tracks the same imbalance across a wider 25% range, which moves more slowly and reflects structural shifts.

What to look for: Sustained green (positive) readings suggest passive buyers are building walls, creating a supportive environment for longs. Sustained red (negative) readings suggest sellers are stacking resistance. The strongest setups occur when the 5% reading flips green while the 25% line is already positive or rising — this signals both near-term and structural support aligning. When both are deeply negative, it often precedes further downside or signals risk of failed breakout attempts.
Liquidity Depth Map
Who controls each price level? Green = bid support (left), Red = ask resistance (right). The colored dot shows who dominates.
How to read this chart: Each row is a price level. Green bars extending left show accumulated bid (buy) liquidity. Red bars extending right show accumulated ask (sell) liquidity. The colored dot on each row shows the net winner at that level: green dot = buyers dominate, red dot = sellers dominate, size reflects how lopsided the imbalance is.

What to look for: Rows below the current price with large green bars and green dots = strong support floor. Rows above price with large red bars and red dots = resistance ceiling. The best long setups show thick green below, thinning red above, and green dots clustering below price. If you see red dots appearing at levels below price, it means sellers are building positions inside the support zone — a warning sign that support may not hold.
Wall Strength Scanner
Which price levels have the most persistent liquidity across all 5 exchanges? Persistence-weighted: walls that survive multiple snapshots rank higher.
How to read this chart: Each row is a price level. Green bars extending left show how persistently bid walls have appeared at that level across snapshots. Red bars extending right show persistent ask walls. Wider bars = more reliable walls (appeared many times across many snapshots), not just large one-time orders that may be spoofing.

What to look for: Levels with wide green bars below price = reliable support. Wide red bars above price = tested resistance. If a level shows strong persistence on both sides, it is a contested zone where significant activity is expected. Thin bars may indicate fleeting liquidity or market-maker repositioning.
